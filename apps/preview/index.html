A skin for Video.js v10

Every play is a take.

Slate dresses the player as a film-set clapperboard. Press play and the sticks clap, the timecode starts counting frames, and the film strip rolls.

Try it: press play, scrub the strip, then let it run to the end.

Call sheet Design notes

  1. Sc. 01

    The opening slate

    Before the first play, a full clapperboard sits on the poster. Hit play: the stick claps, one soft sync flash fires, and the card drops out of frame.

  2. Sc. 02

    A board, not a bar

    Controls live on a solid ink slate with chalk type, so they read over bright skies and dark interiors alike. One accent, production yellow.

  3. Sc. 03

    Clapsticks with timing

    Hinged at the left, open while paused, snapping shut when playback rolls. The angle adapts to the player width, so the gap always looks right.

  4. Sc. 04

    Camera timecode

    HH:MM:SS:FF, counting frames smoothly between player updates. Screen readers still get the plain time from Video.js.

  5. Sc. 05

    The film-strip timeline

    Sprocket holes punched through track, buffer, and progress. Chapters read as separate shots, with an editor’s flagged playhead.

  6. Sc. 06

    Set details

    A REC/STBY tally light, the title on camera tape, a TAKE counter that ticks with every replay, and a closing “That’s a wrap!”.

Safety brief Made for everyone

  • Every control reachable by keyboard, in visual order, with a visible yellow focus ring.
  • Opaque surfaces keep text and icons legible over any footage.
  • Touch targets grow on phones; layouts adapt from wide screens to 340px portrait.
  • Reduced motion turns off the clap, the flash, and the tally blink.
  • Forced-colors mode falls back to system colors.
